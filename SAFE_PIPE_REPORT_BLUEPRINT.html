2. What to borrow, and what to improve

Borrow these practices

  • A formal academic opening with student/supervisor identities.
  • A short abstract that connects problem, method and present status.
  • An explicit objective/research-gap chapter.
  • A readable architecture figure with labelled inputs and outputs.
  • A dataset description and reproducible preprocessing/method section.
  • Comparative result figures accompanied by interpretation.
  • Separate current achievements and future work.

Improve on these practices

  • Avoid mixing literature-supported expectations with experimentally measured results.
  • Do not claim generalization or architectural superiority without the required evaluation.
  • Avoid contradictory present/future tense: the abstract and implementation status must agree.
  • Condense raw model summaries; use a configuration/hyperparameter table instead of many full-page screenshots.
  • Keep headings, units, figure numbers and page references consistent.
  • Check names, course codes and department wording across every front-matter page.

For example, Reference A's declaration contains inconsistent department wording, while Reference B's supervisor naming differs across its cover, certificate and acknowledgement. These are reasons to verify our own details, not copy the pages wholesale. Both reports also contain broad methodological or novelty statements that should not be transplanted into SAFE-Pipe.

Use the seniors' reports as presentation references, not as proof of institutional rules. Their signatures, roll numbers, subject matter, course codes, datasets, medical metrics and performance claims do not belong in our report.

3. The story our report should tell

Our strongest coherent narrative is a bounded runtime-aware configuration advisor, followed by a controlled investigation of sample fidelity and profiling cost. The research branch is an empirical investigation; it is not a newly validated ML selector that replaced the original model.

Build the baselineMeasure known Dask settings, fit a runtime predictor, calibrate its upper estimate and implement constrained advice.
Identify the limitationFour frozen profiles do not establish broad support. Ask whether sampled executions can supply useful pre-decision information.
Investigate carefullyRun the development screen, isolate mechanisms in Stage A, test samples in Stage B, and preserve worker evidence.
Report the actual decisionCurrent probing repeats metadata choices at higher cost. Keep admission disabled and define the next research question.

Working title and scope

Keep the officially submitted/approved title unless the supervisor approves a change. The submitted title in our existing handoff is “SAFE-Pipe: Failure-Aware Cost and Runtime Optimization of Distributed Data Pipelines under SLA Constraints.” Add an accurate scope subtitle if permitted: “A local Dask advisor and an empirical study of probe fidelity and profiling cost.”

If a revised title is allowed, one suitable option is “SAFE-Pipe: Runtime-Aware Configuration Selection and Probe-Fidelity Analysis for Memory-Capped Local Dask.” This is a suggestion, not a confirmed approved title.

Research questions to organize the chapters

  1. How does the original bounded advisor predict and select among eight known configurations under a deadline and resource preference?
  2. How well do sampled executions preserve full-input configuration behaviour under memory pressure?
  3. Do partition-aware sampling or additional probes add useful decision information beyond simple baselines?
  4. Does that information repay sample-preparation and execution overhead for the declared request/use case?
  5. What can sampled worker-memory records establish, and where do event-time readings expose their limits?

Do not organize the main report as a commit diary. Present design rationale and experiments by question; use Git/data revisions only for reproducibility. The learning guides are explanations for the team, not substitutes for an academic methodology chapter.

SAFE-Pipe sources: current context · submitted versus implemented scope · advisor contract · current feasibility finding.

Additional methodology details to state explicitly

  • The original four profiles each use a fixed dataset; repeats of those inputs are not new datasets.
  • The original model predicts engine runtime; research lifecycle/request timing includes additional phases. Keep the timing contracts separate.
  • Sample preparation, probe execution, selected full execution and unmeasured controller overhead must be distinguished. Medians of separate components do not generally sum to the median total.

  • The repository identifier sort_merge executes a key-based shuffle/index operation and a scalar partition result; do not describe it as a measured second-table merge-sort implementation.

9. Formatting and document-production decisions

The references demonstrate A4 academic reports with formal covers and mostly serif body text. They do not establish a current mandatory DTU template. A sensible provisional draft uses A4, 12-point Times New Roman body, 14–16-point bold chapter headings, consistent spacing and approximately 2.54 cm margins; adjust binding margins and all settings to the actual instructions.

  1. Make an editable master. Use Word styles or a controlled LaTeX source, then export PDF. Do not manually space headings or build contents with typed dots.
  2. Use automatic navigation. Contents, caption numbering, cross-references and page numbers should update from document fields. Cover unnumbered; Roman front matter and Arabic body is a possible convention, not a rule inferred from the samples.
  3. Keep academic identity consistent. Same title, names, roll numbers, department and supervisor wording on cover, declaration, certificate and any submission forms.
  4. Make figures printable. Prefer vector diagrams or sharp exports, readable axes and neutral styles. Tables should repeat headers across pages; avoid isolated captions and unreadable terminal/model-summary screenshots.
  5. Use precise citations. Number papers consistently, cite factual research claims near the sentence and provide complete bibliography metadata. Do not copy the seniors' references into an unrelated topic.
  6. Preserve integrity. Use genuine blank signature/date spaces; no fabricated approvals. No credentials, PATs, private machine paths or large raw logs in screenshots or appendices.
  7. Render and review the final PDF. Check every page for overlap, clipping, widows/orphans, blank pages, table breaks, missing glyphs, wrong figure references and stale contents numbers.

The references are useful enough to choose a draft structure now. Font, line-spacing, binding, mandatory certificates and accepted length should remain provisional until the department/supervisor confirms them.
